HER VOICE · HER JOURNEY

Radhika Menon

41 years · Ovarian cancer · Maharashtra, India

Practical, thoughtful and organised, Radhika believes in asking questions, understanding her choices and taking life one step at a time.

Fictional demonstration story: This is a fictional, AI-generated survivor story and portrait created for demonstration purposes. Radhika is not a real patient, and this story is not an actual patient testimonial or medical evidence.

01 · BEFORE THE DIAGNOSIS

The person everyone could depend on.

Radhika was someone who liked having a plan. Whether she was organising her work schedule, managing family responsibilities or preparing for an upcoming occasion, she found comfort in knowing what needed to be done.

She was thoughtful and dependable, often remembering the small details that made life easier for the people around her. She valued meaningful conversations and enjoyed spending time with her family.

Her days were usually busy, divided between work, household responsibilities and the everyday commitments that kept everything moving.

She was accustomed to finding solutions. When something went wrong, her instinct was to understand the problem and work out the next step.

02 · THE DIAGNOSIS

When the questions became too important to ignore.

Radhika began noticing persistent changes in her health. At first, she tried to fit them around her daily responsibilities, hoping they would settle with time.

When the concerns continued, she decided to seek medical advice. What followed was a series of appointments, investigations and conversations that eventually led to an ovarian cancer diagnosis.

The diagnosis was difficult to absorb. She thought about her family, her work and the responsibilities she had always tried to manage independently.

She also wondered how long it would take to understand what was happening and what the next steps would involve.

The experience made her realise how important it was to have her concerns taken seriously and to receive clear explanations throughout the diagnostic process.

03 · THE TREATMENT JOURNEY

Learning that asking questions is part of care.

Treatment introduced a new structure to Radhika's life. Appointments, medical discussions and recovery periods required her to reconsider how she managed work and family responsibilities.

Initially, she found it difficult to step away from the role of being the person who organised everything. There were responsibilities she worried about leaving unfinished, even when she needed time to focus on herself.

Gradually, she began keeping a record of her appointments, medical reports and questions. She wanted to understand the purpose of investigations, what different findings meant and which decisions needed further discussion.

Not every conversation brought immediate clarity. Sometimes she needed to ask the same question in a different way or take time to understand the information she had received.

She learned that she could ask for explanations, discuss her concerns and express her preferences. Being organised was no longer only about managing responsibilities; it also became a way of participating in her own healthcare.

"I could not control every part of what was happening, but I could ask questions and make sure my voice was part of the conversation."

04 · COMPLETING ACTIVE TREATMENT

A new chapter with its own adjustments.

Completing active treatment was a significant moment for Radhika. It gave her an opportunity to pause, reflect and consider how she wanted to approach the next stage of life.

She understood that the end of active treatment did not mean the end of healthcare needs. Follow-up appointments and continued communication with her medical team remained important.

She also had to reconsider the pace at which she returned to everyday responsibilities. Instead of immediately trying to manage everything as before, she began thinking about what was realistic and what support she could continue to accept.

05 · LIFE AFTER TREATMENT

Finding a different kind of balance.

Life after active treatment brought a gradual return to familiar routines. Radhika began making space for work, family activities and the personal interests she had missed.

She became more conscious of how much she expected from herself. There were days when her energy and confidence did not match her plans, and she learned to adjust without treating every change as a failure.

She also found herself approaching responsibilities differently. Delegating tasks and allowing family members to participate became less uncomfortable with time.

Her experience had changed the way she understood balance. It was not about doing everything perfectly. It was about recognising what mattered and making room for her own wellbeing alongside the needs of others.

06 · WHAT HELPED HER

Clarity, shared decisions and a support system.

Radhika valued clear communication with her healthcare team. Having information explained in a way she could understand helped her prepare for appointments and participate in discussions.

Her family also became an important part of her support system. Sharing responsibilities allowed her to focus on her needs without feeling that everything depended on her.

She found that keeping organised records, writing down questions and taking someone she trusted to important conversations could make unfamiliar situations feel more manageable.

Most importantly, she learned that support did not have to mean giving up control. It could mean having people beside her while she remained involved in decisions about her life.

07 · A MESSAGE FROM RADHIKA

Your concerns deserve attention.

Radhika's fictional journey highlights the importance of listening to persistent health concerns and seeking appropriate medical evaluation.

It also reminds us that patients deserve clear information, opportunities to ask questions and meaningful involvement in decisions about their healthcare.

Families and workplaces can make a difference by recognising that a person going through cancer treatment may need practical support as well as emotional understanding.

Asking questions is not being difficult. Needing help is not a failure. And taking care of yourself deserves to be part of the plan too.
