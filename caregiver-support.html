Support for You, Too

When someone you love is diagnosed with cancer, the journey affects you, too. Whether you're a family member, friend or caregiver, you may find yourself navigating unfamiliar responsibilities, difficult emotions and uncertain moments.

This space offers guidance on supporting someone with cancer while also recognising the importance of your own wellbeing. You deserve care and support, too.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I support someone with cancer without overwhelming them?

Start by asking what kind of help they would find useful. Listen to their preferences, respect their decisions and offer specific help rather than assuming what they need. Remember that their needs may change over time.

Is it normal to feel exhausted or overwhelmed as a caregiver?

Caregiving can involve emotional, physical and practical demands. Feeling tired, worried or overwhelmed does not mean you are failing. Make room for rest and seek help from trusted people or a qualified professional when needed.

What should I do if I don't know how to answer the patient's questions?

You do not need to have every answer. Be honest about what you don't know and, when appropriate, help the patient write down questions for their healthcare team. Avoid guessing about medical information.

How can family members share caregiving responsibilities?

Discuss the tasks that need attention and ask family members or friends to take on specific responsibilities. This could include transportation, meals, errands, appointment support or household tasks. Agree on arrangements with the patient's preferences in mind.

When should a caregiver seek professional support?

Consider reaching out to a healthcare professional, counsellor or mental health professional if stress, anxiety, sadness or exhaustion becomes difficult to manage or affects everyday life. You deserve support, too.

Where can I find help if I cannot manage everything alone?

You can explore trusted cancer organisations, caregiver support groups, counselling services and community resources. Visit our Trusted Organisations and Helplines page to explore available support.

You Don't Have to Do This Alone

Supporting someone with cancer doesn't mean forgetting yourself. You deserve time to rest, space to express your feelings and people who support you, too. Remember that caring for yourself is also an important part of being there for someone else.
