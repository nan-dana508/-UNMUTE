HER VOICE · HER JOURNEY

Kavya Sharma

32 years · Breast cancer · Jaipur, Rajasthan, India

Sociable, expressive and deeply connected to her loved ones, Kavya is learning to recognise strength in accepting care as well as giving it.

Fictional demonstration story: This is a fictional, AI-generated survivor story and portrait created for demonstration purposes. Kavya is not a real patient, and this story is not an actual patient testimonial or medical evidence.

01 · BEFORE THE DIAGNOSIS

A life filled with people and little celebrations.

Kavya was someone who rarely let a family gathering pass without finding a reason to celebrate. She enjoyed spending time with friends, trying new places, sharing stories and being part of the everyday moments that brought people together.

Her work kept her busy, but she made an effort to stay connected with the people she loved. She was expressive, warm and usually the first person to suggest a weekend plan.

Like many women in their thirties, she was balancing personal ambitions, relationships and responsibilities. She imagined her future in terms of experiences she wanted to have, places she wanted to visit and memories she hoped to make.

02 · THE DIAGNOSIS

News that changed the way she saw the future.

When Kavya noticed a change in her breast, she decided to seek medical advice. Further investigations led to a breast cancer diagnosis.

The news left her struggling to process several emotions at once. She thought about her family, her work and all the plans she had made. She also found herself worrying about what treatment might mean for her body and sense of identity.

Questions seemed to arrive faster than answers. She wanted to understand her diagnosis, the investigations ahead and the treatment options her healthcare team discussed with her.

It took time for the reality of the diagnosis to settle. She did not always feel ready to talk about it, even with people close to her.

03 · THE TREATMENT JOURNEY

Allowing herself to be cared for.

Treatment brought changes to Kavya's routine and the way she experienced everyday life. Medical appointments and recovery periods required adjustments at work and at home.

One of her more personal challenges involved body image. Changes in appearance and the uncertainty surrounding her body sometimes affected how she felt about herself.

There were moments when she missed feeling carefree and confident. She also found it difficult to accept help at first. She had been accustomed to being the person others depended on, not the person asking for support.

Gradually, she began allowing friends and family to help in practical ways. Some days that meant accompanying her to appointments. On others, it meant sharing a meal, listening without offering advice or simply keeping her company.

She learned that she could be independent and still lean on others when she needed to.

"I spent so much time trying to be strong for everyone else. I eventually learned that I deserved care too."

04 · COMPLETING ACTIVE TREATMENT

Recognising how far she had come.

Completing active treatment was a meaningful milestone for Kavya. It brought relief and an opportunity to look towards the next stage of her life.

At the same time, she realised that reaching this milestone did not automatically resolve every emotion or concern. Adjusting to changes in her body and navigating follow-up care remained part of her experience.

She began to understand that she did not have to rush into feeling like her old self. She could acknowledge what had changed while also making room for new experiences.

05 · LIFE AFTER TREATMENT

Getting comfortable with a changing sense of self.

Life after active treatment brought a mixture of familiar routines and new adjustments. Kavya gradually reconnected with work, friendships and activities she enjoyed.

Body image remained a personal and sometimes complicated part of her experience. She learned that confidence did not always return in a straight line, and that she was allowed to have days when she felt uncertain.

She also became more intentional about the relationships in her life. Instead of always being the one organising everything or checking on everyone else, she began making space for others to show up for her.

Slowly, she found joy in ordinary moments again: meeting a friend, laughing over a familiar story and making plans without feeling that every part of her identity had to be explained through cancer.

06 · WHAT HELPED HER

People who listened without trying to fix everything.

Kavya valued the people who allowed her to express difficult emotions without asking her to stay positive all the time.

Her family and friends helped her feel supported, while honest conversations with her healthcare team helped her understand her care and ask questions about concerns that mattered to her.

She also learned that emotional wellbeing and body image deserved attention. These concerns were not less important simply because active treatment had ended.

07 · A MESSAGE FROM KAVYA

You are allowed to receive the care you give others.

Kavya's fictional journey reminds us that breast cancer can affect more than physical health. It can influence confidence, relationships, work and the way a person sees herself.

Survivors deserve space to talk about these experiences without feeling pressured to appear strong or grateful every moment.

Accepting help does not take away independence. Asking for emotional support is not a weakness. And a woman's identity is never limited to the changes cancer brings.

She is still allowed to laugh loudly, make plans, change her mind and enjoy life in her own way.
