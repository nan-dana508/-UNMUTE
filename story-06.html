HER VOICE · HER JOURNEY

Leela Das

63 years · Ovarian cancer · West Bengal, India

Thoughtful, resilient and deeply connected to her family, Leela is learning that independence does not mean facing every difficult moment alone.

Fictional demonstration story: This is a fictional, AI-generated survivor story and portrait created for demonstration purposes. Leela is not a real patient, and this story is not an actual patient testimonial or medical evidence.

01 · BEFORE THE DIAGNOSIS

A life built around familiar rhythms.

Leela had spent much of her life creating a home filled with familiar routines, meaningful relationships and small traditions. She valued her independence and took pride in managing her responsibilities.

Her days were shaped by family conversations, household routines and the quiet satisfaction of knowing that things were in order. She enjoyed having people around, but also treasured moments of peace and time to herself.

She was not someone who asked for help easily. Having looked after others for so many years, she was accustomed to being capable and dependable.

Growing older had brought its own changes, but she still had plans, interests and plenty of things she wanted to experience. She did not see her age as a reason to stop looking forward.

02 · THE DIAGNOSIS

An unexpected chapter.

When Leela began experiencing persistent changes in her health, she sought medical attention. Further evaluation eventually led to an ovarian cancer diagnosis.

The news was difficult to absorb. She had questions about what would happen next, how treatment might affect her everyday life and whether she would be able to maintain her independence.

Her family was concerned and wanted to protect her. Their support mattered deeply, but Leela also wanted to remain involved in conversations about her own health and decisions.

She understood that accepting support did not mean giving up her voice. She wanted her concerns to be heard, her questions answered and her preferences respected.

03 · THE TREATMENT JOURNEY

Learning to receive care without losing independence.

Treatment introduced unfamiliar routines into Leela's life. Medical appointments, discussions with healthcare professionals and periods of rest required adjustments to the independence she valued.

Some days were more demanding than others. She had to become comfortable acknowledging when she was tired and when a little assistance would make things easier.

Her family gradually learned that supporting her did not mean making every decision on her behalf. They began finding ways to involve her in conversations, listen to her preferences and help without taking away her sense of control.

Leela also recognised the importance of discussing her concerns with her healthcare team. She wanted information explained clearly and needed enough time to understand her options and ask questions.

The experience reminded her that good support should respect a person's age, individuality and wishes rather than make assumptions about what they can or cannot manage.

"I may need help with some things, but I still want to make my own choices. Those two things can exist together."

04 · COMPLETING ACTIVE TREATMENT

A milestone worth acknowledging.

Completing active treatment marked an important point in Leela's journey. She felt grateful to have reached it and appreciated the people who had stood beside her.

She also understood that follow-up appointments and ongoing care remained important. Reaching this milestone did not mean every concern had disappeared or that the future suddenly became predictable.

Instead of rushing herself into a new routine, she allowed time to recognise what she had been through and consider what she wanted the next chapter to look like.

05 · LIFE AFTER TREATMENT

Making room for life beyond cancer.

Gradually, Leela began reconnecting with familiar parts of everyday life. She found comfort in routines that felt manageable and in spending time with the people closest to her.

She continued to value her independence, but became more willing to accept practical help when she needed it. She discovered that receiving care did not make her any less capable or diminish the life she had built.

Her experience also changed the way she thought about ageing and healthcare. She believed older women should never be made to feel that their symptoms, questions or treatment preferences mattered less because of their age.

There were still ordinary things she looked forward to: conversations with family, familiar celebrations and the freedom to make plans at her own pace.

Cancer had become part of her story, but it was not the only thing that defined her. She remained a woman with preferences, relationships, memories and a future she wanted to shape for herself.

06 · WHAT HELPED HER

Support that respected her choices.

Leela valued family members who listened rather than assumed they knew what she needed. Their willingness to include her in decisions helped preserve her sense of independence.

Clear conversations with her healthcare team helped her understand the care process and raise concerns without feeling rushed.

She also found comfort in maintaining familiar routines whenever possible. Small moments of normality reminded her that there was still a life beyond appointments and medical conversations.

07 · A MESSAGE FROM LEELA

Your age does not make your voice smaller.

Leela's fictional journey highlights the importance of dignity, independence and respectful support for older women navigating cancer.

Every woman deserves to have her concerns taken seriously, receive understandable information and participate in decisions about her own care.

Family support can be invaluable, but it should not replace a woman's right to express her wishes and make informed choices.

Life does not stop having possibilities at a particular age. There is room for new experiences, meaningful relationships and personal goals at every stage of life.

A woman's story is always bigger than her diagnosis.
